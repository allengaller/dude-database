# New York Spa Guide

**Data Note**: This guide is compiled from publicly available information.

---

## Overview

New York City offers a wide range of spa experiences, from luxury hotel spas in Manhattan to expansive day spas with thermal circuits. Japanese-inspired bathing rituals, Korean-style saunas, and high-end wellness treatments are all well represented. Prices are among the highest in the US but reflect the caliber of service and facilities.

## Recommended Venues

### Aire Ancient Baths
- **Address**: 88 Franklin St, Tribeca, Manhattan
- **Highlights**: Thermal bath circuit set in a restored 1883 textile factory. Includes caldarium, tepidarium, frigidarium, and flotarium saltwater pool. Candlelit underground atmosphere.
- **Price Range**: $100–$200+ (baths); packages $150–$400
- **Transport**: 1 train to Franklin St

### Shibui Spa (The Greenwich Hotel)
- **Address**: 377 Greenwich St, Tribeca, Manhattan
- **Highlights**: Intimate Japanese-style spa within the Greenwich Hotel. Features a lantern-lit indoor pool fed by a waterfall, shiatsu treatments, and a serene saltstone sauna. One of the most atmospheric spas in the city.
- **Price Range**: $200–$450
- **Transport**: 1 train to Franklin St

### The Spa at Mandarin Oriental
- **Address**: 80 Columbus Circle, 35th Floor, Manhattan
- **Highlights**: Luxury hotel spa overlooking Central Park and the Hudson River. Offers a full range of Asian-inspired treatments, including Thai massage and Ayurvedic rituals. Features a relaxation lounge with panoramic views, steam rooms, and experience showers.
- **Price Range**: $250–$600
- **Transport**: A, B, C, D, 1 trains to 59th St–Columbus Circle

### Great Jones Spa
- **Address**: 29 Great Jones St, NoHo, Manhattan
- **Highlights**: Three-story day spa known for its water lounge with sauna, steam room, and a crystal-jammed hot tub with a waterfall. Offers massages, facials, and body treatments in a laid-back but stylish setting.
- **Price Range**: $120–$350
- **Transport**: 6 train to Bleecker St; B, D, F, M to Broadway–Lafayette St

### Ling New York
- **Address**: 262 Mott St, Nolita, Manhattan
- **Highlights**: Founded by Ling Murray, this boutique spa specializes in Thai and deep-tissue massage. Compact and no-frills but highly regarded for the quality of bodywork. A favorite among locals.
- **Price Range**: $120–$250
- **Transport**: 6 train to Spring St; J to Bowery

### Sojo Spa Club
- **Address**: 660 River Dr, Edgewater, NJ
- **Highlights**: Large Korean-style spa and jjimjilbang with multiple floors of baths, saunas (including Himalayan salt, jade, clay, and ice rooms), rooftop pools with Manhattan skyline views, and a food court. Short trip from Manhattan.
- **Price Range**: $60–$100 admission; treatments extra
- **Transport**: Free shuttle from Manhattan (Midtown); NJ Transit bus 158 or ferry to Edgewater

### Red Door Spa (Elizabeth Arden)
- **Address**: Multiple NYC locations (Midtown flagship historically at 691 Fifth Ave — check current status)
- **Highlights**: Iconic beauty and wellness spa brand founded by Elizabeth Arden in 1910. Known for facials, manicures, and signature treatments. Note: locations have fluctuated post-pandemic — verify before visiting.
- **Price Range**: $100–$300
- **Transport**: Varies by location

### exhale Spa
- **Address**: Multiple locations including 150 Central Park South (Park Hyatt) and 575 Broadway (NoLita)
- **Highlights**: Combines a fitness studio (barre, yoga, HIIT) with a full-service spa. Known for healing massages, facials, and acupuncture. Located within luxury hotels and standalone studios.
- **Price Range**: $120–$350
- **Transport**: N, Q, R, W to 57th St–7th Ave (Central Park South); 6 to Spring St (NoLita)

## Tips

- **Book ahead**: Top spas like Aire Ancient Baths and The Greenwich Hotel's Shibui Spa book out weeks in advance, especially on weekends.
- **Arrive early**: Most venues encourage arriving 30–45 minutes before your treatment to enjoy facilities (thermal baths, steam rooms, lounges).
- **Sojo Spa Club pro tip**: Go on a weekday for smaller crowds. The rooftop pool with Manhattan views is the highlight.
- **Gratuity**: 18–20% is standard for spa treatments in New York, typically added on top of listed prices.
- **Hotel spa access**: Some hotel spas (e.g., Mandarin Oriental) allow non-guests to book treatments but may restrict facility access to hotel guests — confirm when booking.
