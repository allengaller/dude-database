# New York Coffee Guide

**Data Note**: This guide is compiled from publicly available information.

---

## Overview

New York City's specialty coffee scene is one of the best in the world, with third-wave roasters, cozy neighborhood cafes, and pour-over focused bars spread across all five boroughs. Whether you want a meticulously prepared single-origin drip or a quick excellent espresso, the city delivers. Most specialty shops open early (7–8 AM) and serve pastries from notable local bakeries.

## Recommended Venues

### Blue Bottle Coffee
- **Address**: Multiple locations including 450 W 15th St (Chelsea), 1 Rockefeller Plaza (Midtown), 10 S. William St (FiDi)
- **Highlights**: Oakland-originated specialty roaster known for its clean, precise pour-over coffee and New Orleans-style iced coffee. Chelsea outpost is a bright, minimalist flagship. Consistent quality across all NYC locations.
- **Price Range**: $5–$9
- **Transport**: A, C, E to 14th St (Chelsea); B, D, F, M to 47–50th Sts (Rock Center)

### Stumptown Coffee Roasters
- **Address**: 18 W 29th St (NoMad), 30 W 8th St (Greenwich Village)
- **Highlights**: Portland-based roaster with a strong NYC presence. The NoMad location features a full brew bar and a beautiful open space. Known for Hair Bender blend and single-origin offerings. The Village outpost is smaller but equally excellent.
- **Price Range**: $4–$8
- **Transport**: N, Q, R, W to 28th St (NoMad); A, B, C, D, E, F, M to W 4th St (Village)

### Joe Coffee
- **Address**: Multiple locations including 9 E 13th St (Union Square), 405 W 37th St (Hudson Yards), 131 W 21st St (Chelsea)
- **Highlights**: New York-born specialty roaster founded in 2003. One of the city's original third-wave shops. Offers reliably excellent espresso drinks and house-roasted beans. The Union Square location is a local favorite for people-watching.
- **Price Range**: $4–$7
- **Transport**: 4, 5, 6, L, N, Q, R, W to 14th St–Union Sq

### La Colombe
- **Address**: Multiple locations including 270 Lafayette St (NoLita), 443 W 18th St (Chelsea), 400 W 37th St (Hudson Yards)
- **Highlights**: Philadelphia-founded roaster with a major NYC footprint. Famous for its Draft Latte served from a tap system. The NoLita flagship was one of its earliest and most atmospheric shops. Also sells widely in grocery stores.
- **Price Range**: $4–$8
- **Transport**: 6 to Spring St (NoLita); A, C, E to 14th St (Chelsea)

### Devocion
- **Address**: 69 Grand St (Williamsburg, Brooklyn), 100 Willoughby St (Downtown Brooklyn)
- **Highlights**: Specializes in farm-direct Colombian coffee roasted on-site in Brooklyn. The Williamsburg location is a stunning skylit space with abundant greenery and a repurposed industrial feel. One of the most photogenic cafes in NYC. Beans are roasted within 10 days of harvest.
- **Price Range**: $5–$9
- **Transport**: L to Bedford Ave (Williamsburg); A, C, F, N, Q, R to Jay St–MetroTech (Downtown Brooklyn)

### Partners Coffee
- **Address**: 223 Bedford Ave (Williamsburg, Brooklyn)
- **Highlights**: Williamsburg roaster known for carefully sourced beans and a relaxed, sun-filled cafe with communal seating. Their "Tropical Weather" blend is a standout. One of the best roasters operating out of Brooklyn.
- **Price Range**: $4–$8
- **Transport**: L to Bedford Ave; G to Metropolitan Ave

### Abraço
- **Address**: 86 E 7th St, East Village, Manhattan
- **Highlights**: Tiny, legendary East Village institution. No seating — it's strictly a walk-up counter. Known for its olive oil cake, strong espresso, and dedicated following. Opened in 2007 and remains one of the most respected coffee shops in the city.
- **Price Range**: $3–$6
- **Transport**: 6 to Astor Pl; B, D, F, M to Broadway–Lafayette St

### Everyman Espresso
- **Address**: 301 W Broadway (SoHo), 136 E 13th St (Union Square)
- **Highlights**: No-nonsense specialty espresso bar with a loyal following. The SoHo location is a narrow, bustling spot popular with the creative-industry crowd. Known for well-pulled shots and excellent cortados.
- **Price Range**: $4–$7
- **Transport**: 1 to Houston St (SoHo); 4, 5, 6, L, N, Q, R, W to 14th St–Union Sq

### Birch Coffee
- **Address**: Multiple locations including 56 7th Ave (Park Slope, Brooklyn), 214 Sullivan Pl (Prospect Heights, Brooklyn)
- **Highlights**: Founded in 2009 with a focus on approachability and community. Now primarily Brooklyn-based. Known for friendly baristas, solid espresso, and a welcoming atmosphere. Also sells a popular cold brew in retail.
- **Price Range**: $4–$7
- **Transport**: B, Q to 7th Ave (Park Slope); 2, 3 to Grand Army Plaza (Prospect Heights)

### Gregorys Coffee
- **Address**: Multiple Manhattan locations including 31 E 16th St (Union Square), 88 Park Pl (Tribeca), 360 Lexington Ave (Murray Hill)
- **Highlights**: NYC-founded chain with dozens of locations across Manhattan. Known for a bright, modern aesthetic and consistent espresso. While more ubiquitous than artisanal, it reliably delivers above-average coffee. Also offers matcha, chia lattes, and a full food menu.
- **Price Range**: $4–$8
- **Transport**: 4, 5, 6, L, N, Q, R, W to 14th St–Union Sq (flagship)

## Tips

- **Peak hours**: Most cafes are packed 8–10 AM on weekdays and 10 AM–1 PM on weekends. Mid-afternoon is usually the quietest time.
- **Laptop policies**: Many specialty shops (especially Abraço, Everyman) do not allow laptops or have limited seating. Joe Coffee and Gregorys are more laptop-friendly.
- **Cashless**: Most specialty coffee shops in NYC are cashless — bring a card.
- **Brooklyn worth the trip**: Devocion and Partners Coffee in Williamsburg are among the best coffee experiences in the city and justify a trip across the bridge.
- **Pastries**: Blue Bottle (Chelsea) often carries pastries from Ovenly; Joe Coffee partners with local bakeries. Ask the barista.
